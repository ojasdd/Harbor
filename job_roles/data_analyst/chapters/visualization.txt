DATA VISUALIZATION

Data visualization communicates insights from data through charts,
graphs, and dashboards. Good visualizations are clear, accurate, and
tell a story the audience can understand quickly.

---

CHOOSING THE RIGHT CHART

Bar chart: compare values across categories.
Use when: comparing sales by region, headcount by department.

Line chart: show trends over time.
Use when: monthly revenue, daily active users over a year.

Scatter plot: show relationship between two numerical variables.
Use when: age vs salary, advertising spend vs revenue.

Histogram: show distribution of a single numerical variable.
Use when: distribution of customer ages, distribution of order values.

Pie chart: show proportions of a whole.
Use when: market share breakdown. Avoid when there are many categories.

Box plot: show distribution, median, IQR, and outliers.
Use when: comparing salary distributions across departments.

Heatmap: show magnitude of values in a matrix using color.
Use when: correlation matrices, website traffic by hour and day.

Funnel chart: show drop-off through sequential stages.
Use when: sales pipeline, conversion funnel.

DASHBOARDS

A dashboard consolidates multiple KPIs and charts into a single view
for monitoring business performance in real time or near real time.

Good dashboard principles:
- Show the most important metrics prominently
- Use consistent colors and fonts
- Avoid clutter — every element must earn its place
- Design for the audience — executives vs analysts need different views
- Include filters for interactivity

Common dashboard tools:
Tableau: drag and drop, strong visualizations, widely used in enterprise.
Power BI: Microsoft ecosystem, good for Excel users, strong DAX language.
Looker: code-based (LookML), good for data teams, built on SQL.
Google Data Studio: free, connects to Google products easily.
Metabase: open source, easy for non-technical users.

KPI REPORTING

KPIs (Key Performance Indicators) are measurable values that track
progress toward business objectives.

Characteristics of good KPIs:
- Specific and clearly defined
- Measurable with available data
- Actionable — something the team can influence
- Relevant to business goals
- Time-bound — tracked over a specific period

Common data analyst KPIs by domain:

Sales: revenue, conversion rate, average deal size, sales cycle length
Marketing: CAC (customer acquisition cost), CTR, ROAS, churn rate
Product: DAU/MAU, retention rate, NPS, feature adoption rate
Operations: SLA compliance, error rate, processing time, cost per unit

STORYTELLING WITH DATA

Data storytelling combines data, visuals, and narrative to drive decisions.

Structure of a good data story:
1. Context — what is the business question?
2. Findings — what does the data show?
3. Insight — why does it matter?
4. Recommendation — what should be done?

Common mistakes to avoid:
- Truncated Y-axis exaggerates differences
- Using 3D charts distorts perception
- Too many colors confuse the viewer
- Missing axis labels or units
- Showing too much data at once without a clear point

Preattentive attributes draw attention instantly before conscious thought:
color, size, position, shape. Use these to highlight what matters most.
